Grade 8 History
The Indian Ocean Trade Network, 800-1500 CE

Source A: Between approximately 800 and 1500 CE, merchants utilised the predictable seasonal reversal of the monsoon winds to traverse the Indian Ocean. Dhows constructed from teak and coconut fibre transported commodities including frankincense, pearls, textiles and spices between the Arabian Peninsula, East Africa and South Asia. Ports such as Kilwa, Calicut and Hormuz flourished as cosmopolitan entrepots where linguistic and religious exchange accompanied commerce.

Task 1: Comprehension
a) Identify two commodities transported by dhows.
b) Explain why the monsoon winds were significant for merchants.
c) Name one port mentioned in Source A.

Task 2: Analysis
Using Source A, assess the extent to which trade facilitated cultural exchange. Write at least 150 words.

Key vocabulary: entrepot, commodity, monsoon, cosmopolitan
