Worksheet 4.2: Photosynthesis and the Factors Affecting Its Rate

Background Information
Photosynthesis is the endothermic process by which green plants synthesise glucose from carbon dioxide and water, utilising light energy absorbed by the pigment chlorophyll, which is located within the chloroplasts of palisade mesophyll cells. Oxygen is liberated as a by-product and diffuses out of the leaf through the stomata. The overall reaction may be summarised by the word equation: carbon dioxide + water -> glucose + oxygen.

The rate of photosynthesis is constrained by whichever factor is in shortest supply; this is termed the limiting factor. In an investigation, a student placed a sample of pondweed (Elodea) 10 cm from a lamp and counted 24 oxygen bubbles in 1 minute. When the lamp was moved to 40 cm, only 6 bubbles were counted per minute. Temperatures above approximately 45 degrees Celsius cause the enzymes involved to denature, so the rate decreases sharply.
